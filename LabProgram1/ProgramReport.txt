CSC 223 — Program 2: Grid Racers
Author: Kryssa Oxley
Date: 05 NOV 2025

Overview
This program builds on Program 1 by introducing cars that race across a weighted racetrack toward the finish line.
Each car’s movement depends on its velocity, which increases after every move, simulating acceleration on the grid.
The race runs in rounds, updating the racetrack and displaying car statistics until a car reaches the finish.

Specifications Completed
• Cars start at the highest weights available:
  The program scans the racetrack’s 2D weights array to locate all cells with the maximum weight and places one car per cell.
• Cars move to the lowest weight in range:
  For each round, a car examines all reachable cells within its velocity rectangle (±rowVel, ±colVel) and selects the unoccupied cell with the smallest weight.
• Velocities increase each turn:
  After a move, row velocity and column velocity are incremented by the number of spaces traveled vertically and horizontally.
• Cars do not share spaces:
  Occupancy and reservation arrays prevent cars from colliding or occupying the same cell.
• Cars move in assigned order:
  Car1, Car2, Car3 always move sequentially each round, following the order they were created.
• A car wins by reaching the finish:
  The race immediately stops when any car lands on ‘F’, prints the final grid and statistics, and announces the winner.

Topics from Unit 2 (Chapters 4, 6, 8, 9)
• Chapter 4 – Algorithm design and iteration: cars move in a deterministic loop per round.
• Chapter 6 – 2D arrays and matrices: racetrack weights and occupancy grids.
• Chapter 8 – Classes, objects, and encapsulation: CarAgent adapts Car.java to include runtime state (position, velocities).
• Chapter 9 – Comparators and ordering: cars are ranked each round by weight for race placement display.

Program Design
- Racetrack.java now encapsulates everything from Program 1’s driver, including reading the track, placing cars, and managing turns.
- Program2.java serves as the main entry point and orchestrates the simulation.
- CarAgent.java tracks velocity, position, and finish status for each car, leaving the provided Car.java unchanged.

Testing
Tracks used for verification:
1. track1.txt – Wide grid with two finish lines (FF) near the top right.
   Verified cars start at maximum weights and navigate toward the finish in order, without overlap.
2. track2.txt – Narrow vertical racetrack with one finish row (FFF).
   Confirmed that acceleration and tie-breaking operate correctly in a constrained environment.

Enjoyed
Designing and watching the race evolve round-by-round was enjoyable, especially observing velocity growth and tie resolution.

Difficulties
The most challenging part was balancing the movement range so cars progress toward the finish without skipping large sections.
Managing grid occupancy between multiple cars required careful sequencing and debugging.
